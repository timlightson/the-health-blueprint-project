# The Health Blueprint — Brand & Social

## The one promise

Everything the brand makes points at a single line:

> **Health lessons school never taught you.**

Every Instagram carousel answers ONE question and hands off to the site. Keep posts
lighter and more focused than dense info-dumps. Recurring hook, like
@astra_athletic_performance.

Question bank (one per post):
Why am I always tired? · Why do headaches happen? · Is breakfast actually important? ·
Why do I wake up groggy? · Why do I crave sugar at night? · What does dehydration
actually do? · Why do I get sick after exams? · Why do I forget things when I'm stressed?

## The ecosystem ladder

- **Instagram** → "Here's something you didn't know." (the hook)
- **Website** → "Here's the full explanation." (the interactive labs)
- **App** → "Here's how it applies to your habits."

## Instagram visual style

- Warm off-white **cream paper** background with subtle grain.
- Headlines: heavy **condensed uppercase**, deep navy `#1B2A4A`, key words bolded.
- Body/lists: lighter regular-weight navy, sentence case, flat emoji accents.
- **No AI illustrations of people.** Type-driven + clean data graphics only.
- Corners on every slide: `@thehealthblueprintproject` top-left,
  `thehealthblueprintproject.com` top-right.
- After generating with an image model, proofread spelling and every number; regenerate
  garbled slides.

## POST 01 — Sleep · "Why am I always tired?"

### Caption

Most US high school students report getting less than eight hours of sleep on school
nights. That does not produce one fixed penalty for everyone, but insufficient sleep can
affect attention, reaction time, learning, and mood.

The research is easy to overstate. A laboratory comparison between prolonged wakefulness
and alcohol, for example, cannot turn a weekly sleep schedule into a blood alcohol level.
And a study of adults reading on a bright e-reader for four hours before bed is evidence
about sustained evening light, not a universal rule for every glance at a phone.

The Sleep Blueprint lets you compare schedules in a simplified model. It shows its
assumptions and does not calculate personal impairment. Link in bio.

Sources: CDC Youth Risk Behavior Survey 2023 · Paruthi et al., J Clin Sleep Med 2016 ·
Chang et al., PNAS 2015 · Dawson & Reid, Nature 1997
